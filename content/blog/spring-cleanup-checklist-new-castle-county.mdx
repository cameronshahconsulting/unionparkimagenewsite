**Schedule your spring cleanup in New Castle County between mid-March and early May — after the last hard freezes, but before shrubs fully leaf out and weeds establish.** Crews book out fast once the first warm weekend hits, so the best move is calling in February or early March to get on the schedule. Here's what a proper cleanup includes and why the timing matters.

## Why mid-March to early May is the window

Delaware's last frost typically lands in mid-April, but the cleanup window opens earlier because the work isn't planting — it's clearing and prepping:

- **Cutting back perennials too early** (February) removes the insulating dead growth some beneficial insects overwinter in; cutting too late means slicing into new green shoots. Late March is the sweet spot.
- **Pre-emergent weed control** — if your cleanup includes bed treatment, it needs to go down before soil temperatures hit ~55°F, when crabgrass and annual weeds germinate. In New Castle County that's usually early-to-mid April.
- **Pruning before leaf-out** makes shrub structure visible and cleanup cleaner. (Exception: spring bloomers like azalea, lilac, and forsythia get pruned *after* they flower, or you're cutting off this year's show.)
- **Matted leaves left past April actively kill grass.** Any leaves that overwintered on the lawn need to come off as soon as the ground firms up.

## What a real spring cleanup includes

A drive-by "cleanup" is a leaf blower and 45 minutes. A proper one resets the property. Ours includes:

1. **Full leaf and debris removal** — beds, lawn, corners where winter wind piled everything.
2. **Perennials and grasses cut back** to make room for new growth.
3. **Shrub pruning** — deadwood out, shape restored, nothing sheared into meatballs.
4. **Bed edges re-cut** — the crisp line between lawn and bed is half of what makes a yard look maintained.
5. **Weeding** while weeds are small and roots are shallow.
6. **First mow prep** — sticks, gumballs, and winter debris off the turf.
7. **Complete haul-away.** No curb piles.

Optional but popular add-ons: fresh mulch (2–3 inches, kept off stems), pre-emergent bed treatment, and a first fertilizer application for the lawn.

## What it costs in New Castle County

Most spring cleanups here run **$300–$900**, depending on lot size, bed footage, and how much winter left behind. Heavily treed properties in Brandywine Hundred, Hockessin, or Greenville trend higher; townhome lots in Pike Creek or Bear trend lower. Adding mulch typically brings a combined cleanup-plus-mulch visit to **$1,000–$2,500**. Quotes are free — photos are often enough for us to price it same-day.

## Can you DIY it?

Sure — budget a full weekend or two, a truck for debris, and dump fees. The honest math for most homeowners: the pro cleanup costs about what the rental equipment, bags, dump runs, and two lost weekends do, and it's done in a day. Where DIY genuinely makes sense: small lots with few beds and somewhere to compost.
