**A paver patio in Delaware costs $25–$40 per square foot installed in 2026, so a typical 400-square-foot patio runs $10,000–$16,000.** Smaller walkway projects start around $2,500, while large patios with seating walls and a fire pit commonly reach $18,000–$30,000. Here's where the money actually goes — and where it's safe (and not safe) to save.

## What drives the price

**Size, obviously — but not linearly.** Mobilization, excavation setup, and material delivery cost roughly the same for a 250 sq ft patio as a 400 sq ft one, so bigger patios cost less per square foot.

**The base is a third of the job.** A patio that lasts in Delaware needs 6–8 inches of compacted stone base under the pavers, more in wet clay. That's excavation, hauling, stone, and machine compaction in lifts. When a quote comes in dramatically cheap, this is almost always what got cut — and it's why cheap patios heave and dip within a few winters here. Our clay soil holds water and moves with every freeze-thaw cycle.

**Paver choice swings cost 20–30%.** Standard concrete pavers from Techo-Bloc, EP Henry (a regional PA/DE favorite), or Cambridge run mid-range. Premium slab-style or natural stone like bluestone costs noticeably more per square foot before labor.

**Site access matters.** A backyard you can reach with a skid steer prices very differently from one where every ton of stone travels by wheelbarrow through a 36-inch gate. Tight Wilmington city lots often carry an access premium; open Middletown new-construction lots rarely do.

**Features add up fast.** Seating walls run roughly $150–$300 per linear foot. Fire pit kits installed typically add $2,000–$4,500. Steps, borders in a contrast color, and lighting all add — but they're also what makes a patio feel custom.

## Pavers vs. concrete: the honest math

A poured concrete patio costs less upfront — often $12–$20 per square foot. But concrete in Delaware cracks; it's a question of when, not if, and a cracked slab can't be invisibly repaired. Pavers cost more on day one, flex with freeze-thaw instead of cracking, and any settled section can be lifted and re-set. Over 20 years, the paver patio is usually the cheaper structure. ## How to keep the budget in check

1. **Right-size it.** A well-designed 350 sq ft patio beats an empty 600 sq ft one. Furniture layout determines size — plan the dining set and seating area first.
2. **Spend on the base, save on the paver.** A mid-priced paver on a perfect base outlasts a premium paver on a thin one. Never invert that trade.
3. **Phase the features.** Build the patio now; add the fire pit or pergola next season. Good contractors design for phase two from the start.
4. **Get the quote in writing.** Itemized: excavation depth, base thickness, paver line, edge restraint, and polymeric sand should all be listed. If they're not, ask why.
